How we work

A music company that explains the royalties first

Her Dream Ltd is a private limited company registered in England and Wales under company number 09478332, based in London and working in sound recording and music publishing since March 2015 under the same director. The company has traded under earlier names and the registered history is public at Companies House, which we would rather point to than paraphrase.

What we explain before anything else is how the money actually works. A recorded track carries two separate copyrights: the song, and the recording of the song. They can be owned by different people, they are licensed separately, and their royalties are collected by different organisations — the song through PRS for Music and MCPS, the recording through PPL. An artist who has joined one and not the other is earning royalties that nobody is collecting. Alongside that: an assignment of copyright is only valid in writing and signed, an advance is a recoupable payment and not a fee, and we never charge an artist for finding them work, which the Conduct Regulations restrict and which creates exactly the wrong incentive.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

What are the two copyrights you keep mentioning?

The musical and literary work — the song as written — and the sound recording of it, the master. Two separate copyrights under the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, which can have different owners. The song's performing and mechanical income comes through PRS for Music and MCPS; income from use of the recording comes through PPL. Being a member of one does not get you paid by the other.

Do I have to pay you to be considered or signed?

No, and you should be wary of anyone who asks. We never charge an artist a fee to find them work or engagements, which the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations 2003 restrict. More simply: a company paid upfront is paid whether or not anything happens for you, which removes its reason to tell you the truth about your prospects.

Can we agree a publishing deal on a handshake?

No. An assignment of copyright is only effective if it is in writing and signed, so a verbal publishing deal transfers nothing and leaves everyone unclear about what was meant. We write down what is assigned, the term, the territory and the income split, and we give you a copy.

Is an advance money I keep?

It is yours to use, but it is recoupable: it comes back out of future royalties before you see further income. It is a payment against earnings, not a fee, and anyone presenting it as a signing bonus is being careless or worse. We state the recoupment terms in writing so you know what has to be earned before royalties flow.

Will you get my track playlisted or on the radio?

We cannot promise that and nobody honest will. We can plan a release properly, get the metadata and registrations right so that any income earned can actually be paid, and pitch it. Streams, placements and chart positions depend on decisions made by people none of us control.

Should I get a lawyer to look at your contract?

Yes, and we will say so in writing. We do not give legal advice and an agreement transferring your copyright deserves independent eyes. If a lawyer advises you against signing, that is useful information about the deal rather than an obstacle to it.
