Program 01 · Splits before the session

Recording and production

We settle who owns what before the session rather than after the track does well, because that is when agreement becomes difficult. A producer who contributed to the composition has a claim on the song, not just the recording, and leaving that vague is how co-writers end up in disputes years later over something nobody wrote down in an afternoon.

Program 02 · Written, or it is not valid

Music publishing

An assignment of copyright is only effective in writing and signed, so there is no such thing as a publishing deal agreed by conversation. We write down what is assigned, for how long and for which territories, and we tell artists to take independent legal advice before signing. If that advice talks them out of it, the deal was not good enough.

Program 03 · Both societies, not one

Rights registration

This is the unglamorous work that decides whether money arrives. Performing and mechanical income on the song is collected through PRS for Music and MCPS; income from use of the recording comes through PPL. Separate registrations, separate memberships. Add wrong metadata on a release and the royalty exists but cannot find its owner.

Program 04 · No fee to the artist

Release and artist development

We do not charge an artist a fee to find them work or to be considered, which the law restricts and which we would not do anyway: a company paid upfront has no incentive to be truthful about someone's prospects. We also say when a track is not ready, and when an artist would be better served by another label.
